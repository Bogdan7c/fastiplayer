# Video VAAPI Core

> **Superseded notice (2026-07-03):** любые упоминания hover preview, hover predecode, hover budget/reservation, timeline-hover prepare или hover overlay ниже являются историческими и не описывают активный контракт. Актуальные owners и запреты: `mem:core` и `mem:frame-server/core`. Остальная non-hover информация этой memory остаётся действующей.

- `video-vaapi` owns VA display/probe/decode thread, cros-codecs adapters, VA surfaces, DMA-BUF export, neutral `FrameResourcePool`, and VA surface lifetime until renderer/player release. It must stay free of `wgpu`, `wgpu-types`, `ash`, `render-wgpu-video`, `render-wgpu-shell`, and `player-core` direct dependencies.
- Shared hardware reservation ownership lives inside `video-vaapi::shared_hardware_owner`: V1 is a pure VAAPI-local accounting boundary hosted by the existing decode thread/decoder construction, with raw `Rc<cros_codecs::libva::Display>` still private to the decoder. It creates a typed playback reservation for the active VA decoder and exposes internal hover capability/admission using `frame-server-core::hover_budget` DTOs. Since S30D it also implements `video_backend_api::HoverBudgetDiagnosticsProvider` through backend-local DTO conversion, so `app-egui` can read capability/admission diagnostics without seeing VA owner internals. Hover admission stays separate from capability, allows at most one active hover reservation, requires hover hardware surfaces to be strictly below playback budget and provider capacity, returns typed pressure/unavailable/fatal reports, and hover release/drop must not release playback reservation/surfaces. `hover_admission_report` is read-only; only `reserve_hover_branch`/`admit_hover_reservation` mutates reservation state. No second `VADisplay`, no public player/frame-server VA types, and no UI/executor wiring are part of this boundary.
- Internal codec adapter boundary is a private module family under `crates/video-vaapi/src/codec_adapter/`: `codec_adapter.rs` owns only codec-neutral DTO/error/event/trait facade plus tests, `factory.rs` owns narrow registry and implemented-format policy, while `h264.rs`, `h265.rs`, and `vp9.rs` independently own concrete cros-codecs adapters and codec lifecycle state. It is not public API. H.264/H.265 access-unit conversion, parameter-set injection, pending partial-consumption/retry, and flush reset remain codec-owned; do not introduce a generic shared AU state merely because their method names resemble each other. `VaapiVideoDecoder` owns backend-local queues/resource registration and delegates submit/flush/events/stream-info/reuse intent to a boxed internal `VaapiCodecAdapter`. `VaapiDecodedFrameHandle::surface_ready() -> anyhow::Result<bool>` is the backend-local non-blocking VA surface readiness boundary for future suppressed-surface reclaim; it delegates to cros-codecs `DecodedHandle::try_is_ready()` and must preserve query errors instead of treating them as ready, while raw `VASurfaceStatus` stays below the adapter boundary. Adapter reuse is adapter-owned through `can_reuse_for_config(&VideoStreamDecodeConfig)`: default is `false` for config-sensitive codecs, VP9 explicitly preserves same-codec reuse/no-op semantics, and H.264/H.265 recreate until an exact-config reuse contract exists.
- Implemented adapter paths are VP9, H.264, and H.265 Main/Main10. VP9 wraps `cros_codecs::StatelessDecoder<Vp9, VaapiBackend<InternalVaapiFrame>>` and ignores packet hints. H.264 wraps `cros_codecs::StatelessDecoder<H264, VaapiBackend<InternalVaapiFrame>>` privately, requires configured `VideoStreamDecodeConfig` with H.264 packetization plus non-empty `codec_private`, parses `avcC`, converts access units through `codec-core::h264_access_unit_to_annex_b_into`, and feeds cros one NAL at a time until the access unit is fully consumed. H.264 SPS/PPS injection is driven by codec-neutral internal `VaapiPacketDecodeHints { inject_parameter_sets }` plus adapter lifecycle: inject only for decode-start/keyframe AU or the first AU after configure/flush. H.265 wraps `cros_codecs::StatelessDecoder<H265, VaapiBackend<InternalVaapiFrame>>`, requires `VideoStreamPacketization::H265`, accepts absent/incomplete `hvcC` when packetization/profile contract is otherwise safe, stores known VPS/SPS/PPS from `hvcC` and later in-band AUs, converts through `codec-core::h265_access_unit_to_annex_b_into`, and uses the same pending-AU/backpressure/CheckEvents retry lifecycle. H.265 capability advertisement is enabled only for physically supported Main 8-bit YUV420/NV12 and Main10 10-bit YUV420/P010 through the production capability intersection.
- H.264 adapter backpressure and lifecycle invariants: `CheckEvents` drains events and retries the same unconsumed NAL without double-consume; `NotEnoughOutputBuffers` preserves the pending access unit/offset and returns output backpressure to the decoder thread; terminal parse/decoder/backend errors discard the failed pending AU so a later packet cannot replay stale bytes under a new timestamp; seek flush/reconfigure clear pending input owned by the old stream generation. H.264 configure, explicit flush, and a nonfatal decoder/reference-chain error all arm backend-owned `H264DecodeRecovery`: following inter-frames are ACKed without cros submit until a proven keyframe is accepted; a decode error first performs one codec/resource flush, and recovery-flush failure is fatal. Every consumed nonfatal decode-error packet receives the normal decoder-thread ACK so player in-flight accounting cannot leak; fatal errors remain unacknowledged and use the existing fatal reset boundary. `H264AccessUnitPreparer` owns a reusable Annex B scratch buffer; `H264PendingAccessUnit` owns converted bytes until full consume; only after complete consume are bytes returned to scratch with capacity preserved. Parameter-set injection is codec-neutral at the VAAPI boundary via `VaapiPacketDecodeHints { inject_parameter_sets }`; H.264 interprets it as SPS/PPS, and H.265 interprets the same intent as VPS/SPS/PPS. Explicit EOF/DPB drain is a separate adapter intent (`begin_end_of_stream_drain`) and must fail if an access unit is only partially submitted; the H.264 implementation uses the cros-codecs flush primitive internally only to emit DPB tail events, not as player seek semantics. H.264 output remains NV12/DMA-BUF zero-copy only; CPU decode/upload/readback fallback is not allowed.
- Cros-codecs VAAPI decode backend must create `VAContext` lazily from the real stream sequence/profile/RT format in `new_sequence`, not in `VaapiBackend::new` from a synthetic H.264 Main placeholder. AMD/Mesa rejects that fake startup context with `VaError(19)`, while Intel happened to tolerate it; keeping lazy context creation preserves AMD startup without changing Intel stream-specific context semantics. Codec-specific VAAPI decode methods must obtain the context through the backend boundary (`active_context`) rather than reading the field directly.
- `VaapiVideoDecoder` owns decoder event drain policy. Publish policy turns `FrameReady` into `DecodedFrame`, exports DMA-BUF, queues it in backend `ready_queue`, and assigns the caller-supplied seek generation at event-drain time. DMA-BUF export layout is now selected by the negotiated `VideoFrameContract`, not inferred only from decoded pixel format. The VAAPI provider advertises both composed and separate-layer DMA-BUF layouts for NV12 and P010, while capability scanning keeps only layouts accepted by the active renderer. P010 ordering is driver-aware: Mesa/AMD prefers composed-first, Intel/unknown remains separate-first for compatibility with the older Intel i965 path. `VaapiVideoDecoder` maps its `expected_frame_contract` to `DecodedDmaBufExportLayout` before export and asks cros-codecs for that exact layout; export failure remains a decoder error rather than silently falling back to a different layout. Discard policy is used by seek `flush()` after adapter flush: `FrameReady` handles from H.264 DPB tail are dropped without DMA-BUF export or `ready_queue` insertion, then decoder-owned ready frames are released; frames already handed to player/render remain untouched. `FormatChanged` is handled the same under both policies: release decoder-owned ready frames, invalidate idle resource mappings, and resize the VA output frame pool from adapter `stream_info()`.
- Accurate seek preroll output-floor is implemented inside `VaapiVideoDecoder` and exposed only through neutral `video-core` contracts on `VideoDecodeThread`/`VideoDecoderThreadHandle`. Active floor state tracks generation, floor PTS, retain-latest policy, and whether a target-or-after frame was published. Matching `FrameReady` with `pts < floor` is suppressed without DMA-BUF export or publish, emits `SeekPrerollFrameSuppressed`, and optionally retains only the latest pre-floor `VaapiDecodedFrameHandle` as EOF fallback candidate. Suppressed/candidate drop paths are backend-local reclaim ownership now: `VaapiVideoDecoder` queues dropped suppressed/candidate handles in a bounded `suppressed_reclaim_queue`, normal passes only call `VaapiDecodedFrameHandle::surface_ready()` and return `Ok(true)` handles, `Ok(false)` and query errors stay queued, and blocking `sync()` is reserved for rare overflow/lifecycle forced drain. Decode loop must run a cheap reclaim before submitting a new packet and return `VaapiDecodePacketOutcome::OutputBackpressured` before adapter submit if the queue remains full, preserving packet ACK/accounting. If queue overflow happens during event drain after an accepted packet, the backend may forced-sync only the oldest suppressed handle as a rare fatal-fallback path; forced sync failures are fatal decoder errors, not warnings. Lifecycle boundaries (`flush`, stream reconfigure, format change, output-floor set/clear, decoder drop) must force-drain retained candidate plus queue before old stream/pool state can be invalidated; flush/discard tail frames use sync+return rather than raw drop and log sync latency for Session E. Repeated seek/scrub during active preroll can pay blocking force-drain cost only on lifecycle boundaries; this is an intentional v1 safety trade-off that needs hardware measurement before designing async pending-destroy/detached-destroy. A matching target-or-after frame drops the candidate before normal `process_ready_frame`; target-or-after publish itself still uses the normal sync/export/register/publish path. EOF drain promotes the retained candidate at most once through `process_ready_frame` only when no target-or-after frame was published for the active floor generation. The reclaim bound is derived inside `VaapiDecoderRuntimeConfig` from surface pool accounting and can be varied for validation through the backend-local env override `RUSTIPLAYER_VAAPI_MAX_SUPPRESSED_RECLAIM_FRAMES`; do not add `video-core`/TOML API for this without an explicit architecture decision. Session E hardware validation relies on backend-local debug/warn logs rather than a neutral `video-core` diagnostic event: reclaim logs must expose queue depth, configured max, approximate available reclaim slots, approximate reserved surface headroom, enqueued suppressed handles, reclaimed count per pass, query errors, ring-full pre-submit backpressure, and forced sync fallback latency/counters. Session E hardware result on Intel i965 Kaby Lake (VA-API 1.23/libva 2.22.0, i965 2.4.1) with default surface pool 24/default reclaim bound 14: manual Accurate seek validation across H.265 118 Mbps, H.265 `ios-hevc-main10-aac-4k60`, VP9 SDR, and H.264 high 180 Mbps resumed after every parsed seek, published target-or-after frames, and produced final commits; no fallback promotions were observed. Reclaim accounting stayed bounded with max depth 1 and enqueued=reclaimed per run (H.265 796/796, ios-hevc-main10-aac-4k60 1036/1036, VP9 2533/2533, H.264 1122/1122); forced sync, ring-full, readiness query errors, `NotEnoughOutputBuffers`, and decoder output-buffer backpressure were all zero. User-visible stalls remained on heavy/random seeks (roughly 0.5-2.0 s), and diagnostics attributed them to `decoder_output@...` delays rather than suppressed-surface reclaim overflow. Bound tuning is partial: default 14 was safe, a bound-8 spot test did not overflow, but no public TOML/user config knob was added or justified.
- `decoder_thread` has internal control messages for `ConfigureStream(VideoStreamDecodeConfig, ack)`, `SetPrerollOutputFloor(VideoPrerollOutputFloor, ack)`, `ClearPrerollOutputFloor(VideoPrerollOutputFloorClear, ack)`, and explicit `BeginEndOfStreamDrain(generation, ack)`. Sender-side `configure_stream` prevalidates and updates cached config only after the decoder thread has recreated the adapter. Preroll floor set/clear returns neutral `VideoPrerollOutputFloorResult`, preserving Applied/Unchanged/Cleared/Backpressure/Fatal; backpressure uses `VideoDecoderControlBackpressureReason::ControlChannelFull`. EOF drain is acknowledged separately from seek flush, publishes tail frames through the normal frame channel, and keeps the player-visible state as `Draining` while backend-ready/pending/channel frames still exist. VAAPI now implements the neutral decoder activity boundary: `VideoDecodeThread::new_with_config` creates `VideoDecoderActivityNotifier`/`Subscription`, the handle stores the subscription and returns `decoder_activity_snapshot()`, and the decoder loop emits non-blocking activity on packet ACK, ready/published frame progress, diagnostic/error events, and EOF drain transitions (`Draining`, `Drained`, `Fatal`). Dropping the activity receiver is not fatal for decoder thread; it remains a typed wait-side state. This keeps cros/VAAPI implementation details inside the backend and avoids exposing VAAPI-specific public `video-core` API.
- `decoder_thread` is split into private child modules under `crates/video-vaapi/src/decoder_thread/`: `control.rs` owns `ThreadControlMsg`, `DecoderControlOperation`, control-channel pressure counters/stats conversion, ACK wait helpers, and control send error mapping; `resource_provider.rs` owns `VideoFrameResourceProvider`, resource lookup descriptor/status enums, lock diagnostics, and resource lookup/release helpers; `runtime_loop.rs` owns decoder-thread runtime orchestration (`decoder_thread_loop`, control draining/handling, queued packet decode, pending frame publish, EOF drain runtime state helpers, and activity/error notification helpers); `tests.rs` owns focused unit tests/helpers/fakes and is connected from `decoder_thread.rs` through private `#[cfg(test)] mod tests;`. This is a refactor-only layout: keep external paths through the parent `decoder_thread` module, do not widen production visibility beyond private child modules/minimal `pub(super)`, and do not change assertions/fake behavior for this split.
- VAAPI stream config validation is centralized through `VaapiCodecAdapterFactory::stream_config_rejection`; `decoder_thread::reject_unsupported_vaapi_stream_config` delegates there. VP9 Profile 0 accepts only 8-bit YUV420/NV12; VP9 Profile 2 accepts only 10-bit YUV420/P010; H.264 accepts Baseline/ConstrainedBaseline/Main/High only as distinct exact 8-bit YUV420/NV12 profiles with valid packetization and `avcC`; H.265 accepts Main 8-bit YUV420/NV12 and Main10 10-bit YUV420/P010 with proven `VideoStreamPacketization::H265`, rejects known 4:2:2/4:4:4/12-bit/SCC variants, parses `hvcC` when present but does not require full VPS/SPS/PPS arrays if in-band parameter sets are allowed. H.264 High10/4:2:2/4:4:4/MVC/Stereo and VP9 Profile 1/3 remain unsupported/future.
- VAAPI probe records raw VA profiles/entrypoints/RT formats for diagnostics, but advertised `supported_video_decode_formats` are filtered through the validated production matrix. H.264 Baseline is advertised only when the driver exposes exact `VAProfileH264Baseline`; ConstrainedBaseline/Main/High keep their own exact rows. Every H.264 row remains 8-bit YUV420 and still requires the hardware profile, zero-copy export and renderer materializer checks. The cros-codecs SPS mapping uses `profile_idc=66` plus `constraint_set1_flag` to distinguish ConstrainedBaseline from ordinary Baseline and never aliases either to Main. H.265 is advertised only for Main 8-bit YUV420 and Main10 10-bit YUV420 when the hardware profile, DMA-BUF export, and renderer materializer/color policy checks pass; AV1/VP8 and unsupported H.264/H.265 variants are not advertised.
- `capability-core::CapabilityScanner` filters available backend formats for the final system report by DMA-BUF export plus registered renderer support. This means advertised system decode formats are the production intersection, not raw hardware capabilities.
- Reconfigure/flush cleanup must release only decoder-owned ready frames. Frames already handed to player/renderer remain guarded and must be released through the normal renderer/player release path.
- Resource-pool mutex poison follows the project fail-closed policy in `mem:panic-invariant-policy`: `VaapiResourcePoolPoisonError` is a backend-local typed fatal decoder error, controlled recovery via `PoisonError::into_inner` is forbidden, reconfigure/format-change stop through the existing decoder error boundary, stats poison marks sticky `DecodeThreadError`, and cleanup must return errors rather than re-panic on the poisoned lock.
- `ZeroCopySurfacePool` keeps bounded storage by live slot state, not by historical VA surface ids. If a new surface id arrives while slots are at capacity, only a `Free` slot may be selected for `ZeroCopyImportPlan::Replace` and remapped after successful import; `Leased`, `WaitingGpuCompletion`, and `WaitingDecoderReuse` slots must remain protected and still produce exhaustion/busy behavior through the existing caller paths. Do not increase `DEFAULT_ZERO_COPY_SURFACE_POOL_SLOTS` to hide stale surface-id churn. Focused regression coverage lives in `zero_copy_surface_pool` and `resource_pool` unit tests.
- `VaapiVideoDecoder` stores `VideoStreamDecodeConfig.display_orientation` as stream metadata and copies it to `DecodedFrame.display_orientation`. VAAPI/cros adapters still decode/export coded surfaces; they must not rotate frames or add WGPU/render dependencies for display orientation.
- `gbm_allocator::allocate_gbm_buffer` is currently covered by a hardware-dependent unit smoke only and is not on the active playback path. The production function must still reject non-linear modifiers for CPU texture upload; `gbm_allocator::tests::test_allocate_gbm_buffer` may skip only the typed `NonLinearGbmBufferError` because AMD/Mesa can report `Modifier::Invalid` for this optional linear allocation even while normal VA-API/Vulkan paths work.


## Safety audit Linear GBM / DMA heap (Session 13, 2026-07-11)

- `LinearGbmDevice` и `LinearGbmVideoFrame` являются строго owner-thread типами: lifetime device/BO связан через `Rc`, оба compile-time `!Send + !Sync`; недоказанные `unsafe impl Send/Sync` удалены. `Rc` гарантирует только порядок lifetime/Drop и не синхронизирует GBM calls.
- Базовый patched `cros_codecs::video_frame::VideoFrame` больше не требует `Send + Sync`: thread traits задаются на реальной C2 command boundary (`C2DecoderWorker`: `Send + Sync` из-за `Arc<V>`, `C2EncoderWorker`: `Send` для owned frame). VA decode path остаётся на owner thread через `Rc<Display>`/`Rc` handles.
- Linear GBM CPU `map`/`map_mut` fail closed: Mesa GBM не документирует достаточный span многоплоскостного mapping для безопасного построения Rust slices. VA-API DMA-BUF zero-copy import, NV12/R8 allocation fallback, surface counts и formats не изменены.
- Drop order: frame уничтожает `gbm_bo` до release последнего `Rc<LinearGbmDevice>`; device вызывает `gbm_device_destroy` до закрытия render-node `File`. cros-libva `Surface` удерживает `Rc<Display>` и external descriptor, поэтому `vaDestroySurfaces` выполняется до возможного `vaTerminate`, а export fd живёт весь surface lifecycle.
- `dma_heap::allocate_dma_buffer` принимает fd 0 как валидный результат ioctl, проверяет `u32 -> RawFd` без truncation и немедленно передаёт exactly-once ownership в `File`. Linear GBM descriptor теперь проверяет plane capacity, pitch/offset и DMA-BUF size перед `u32` conversion; ошибки не маскируются.
- Focused safety coverage: negative assertions для `LinearGbmDevice`, `LinearGbmVideoFrame`, libva `Display`/`Surface`; positive assertions для owned `File` и value-only external descriptor; hardware-optional GBM lifetime/drop/fail-closed mapping test и DMA heap fd conversion/allocation tests.

- Session 25 (2026-07-11) decomposed `crates/video-vaapi/src/decoder.rs` without changing the public decoder contract or VA lifecycle. Private child owners now live under `decoder/`: `config.rs` owns runtime surface/ready/reclaim sizing and validation-only override; `suppressed_reclaim.rs` owns the bounded FIFO, counters, nonblocking readiness passes, and forced FIFO sync/drain fallback through `SuppressedReclaimState`; `preroll.rs` owns accurate-seek floor/counters and fallback-candidate decisions; `event_drain.rs` owns retry reports/policy plus FrameReady/FormatChanged publish-discard orchestration. `decoder.rs` remains the main VA adapter/resource/ready-output/lifecycle coordinator. Focused decoder tests are split under `decoder/tests/` into `config`, `event_drain`, `preroll`, `reclaim`, and `contracts`; shared fakes/helpers stay in `decoder/tests.rs`. Surface pool sizing, ready bounds, reclaim ordering, sync fallback, generation propagation, EOF/flush policy, and decoded frame contract remain unchanged.
