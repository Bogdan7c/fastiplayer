# ThinkPad T410: final qualification of the OpenGL ES 2.0 renderer (2026-10-29)

Session 14 of the GLES2 renderer plan (`user/gles2-renderer/`), measurement protocol
version 4 (`tools/local/README.md`), the same scenes, files and reference modes as the
[2026-10-27 report](t410-gles2-2026-10-27.md). That report measured the renderer before the
optimization sessions 11b–13b; this one measures the build that is published. Sanitized
per-attempt data: [`t410-gles2-2026-10-29.json.gz`](t410-gles2-2026-10-29.json.gz)
(11 series, 323 attempts: the six series of this report, the two A/B runs and the three series
stopped by GPU hangs, with every invalid attempt and its reason).

**Summary.**
- **No frame loss in `dynamic` on any ordinary scene**, 720p30 to 1080p60, under native Wayland
  and XWayland. mpv and VLC lost none either.
- **1080p60 (software decoding for every player): less CPU than both.** Native Wayland:
  114.8 % against mpv 127.2 % and VLC 128.5 % — WIN by 12–14 pp (13.9–16.0 pp with the
  compositor). XWayland: 115.3 % against 119.3 % and 128.0 %, and 26.6 pp below mpv once the
  compositor is counted (mpv `--vo=xv` moves its color conversion into Xwayland).
- **Under XWayland no ordinary scene is WORSE**, and forced hardware decoding is level with the
  references (hardware 1080p30: 12.4 % against 11.6 % and 12.6 %, a WORSE of 0.7 pp against mpv).
  Under native Wayland, hardware decoding at 30 fps cost Fastiplayer about 4–5 pp more than mpv
  and VLC (16 % against 11–12 %), and hardware 1080p30 showed late frames; see
  [Native Wayland at 30 fps](#native-wayland-at-30-fps).
- **720p60 now stays on hardware decoding** in `dynamic` (it moved to software before session
  12a.1) and plays without loss at 22–23 % CPU. With forced hardware decoding on all three
  players, Fastiplayer is level with mpv and VLC (INC, 2.6–4.1 pp above).
- **1080p60 on hardware is impossible on this GPU for every player** (the silicon decodes about
  39 frames per second); `dynamic` moves such files to software after about 6.6 s.
- **The heaviest ladder step, HEVC 1080p60 at 25 Mbit/s (software decoding for everyone):** no
  frame loss and the least CPU. Native Wayland: 224 % against mpv 243 % and VLC 253 % (WIN by
  19 and 29 pp); XWayland: 226 % against 241 % and 253 % (WIN against mpv by 15 pp, 39 pp with
  the compositor). mpv lost 1–9 frames per window, VLC 0–2.

That choice was made when the references lost frames on hardware 720p60 with a system monitor
open. Without it they play hardware 720p60 cleanly (hardware series below), so on the 720p60
row of the ordinary scenes Fastiplayer on hardware decoding is compared with references on
software decoding. **Read that row as "what each player does by default here", not as a
like-for-like efficiency ratio**; the hardware series is the like-for-like comparison.

mpv reports `DR failed` on this GPU (direct rendering needs OpenGL 4.4), so it copies each
software frame. Fastiplayer shows software frames without a CPU copy (sealed memfd →
`/dev/udmabuf` → EGLImage, with a CPU cache flush of the visible rows on this cache-incoherent
GPU). VLC 3 has no native Wayland video output and always draws through XWayland.

## Native Wayland at 30 fps

Two results above are specific to native Wayland at 30 fps with hardware decoding: about
4–5 pp more CPU than mpv and VLC, and, on hardware 1080p30, 110 of 600 new frames shown one
refresh late (none lost). Under XWayland the same build is level with the references (720p30
12.8 % against 11.8–11.9 %; hardware 1080p30 12.4 % against 11.6–12.6 %) and shows no late frames.

Two short A/B runs after the series (same protocol, three Fastiplayer builds as the three
players, native Wayland) checked whether this is a regression of the published code:

| Build | 720p30 HW | 1080p30 HW | 1080p30 HW late frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| session 12a control build (`08b331f5`, before 12a.1) | 15.1 % | 14.7 % | 133 (and 29 lost) |
| session 12a.1 final | 16.2 % | 16.1 % | 106 |
| published build | 16.4 % | 16.6 % | 83 |
| session 12a.2 final / 13b final (first A/B, `dynamic`) | 16.2 % / 16.3 % | — | — |

The published build is not worse than the earlier ones; 12a.1 adds about 1 pp on hardware
30 fps (a known cost, owner decision 75). But the **same 12a control build measured 11.5 % on
720p30 on 2026-10-27** and 15.1 % here, and the session 12a.2 runs on 2026-10-28 measured
hardware 1080p30 without late frames. No package changed on the machine in between; mpv and VLC
measured the same as before. The cause of this native-Wayland difference between days is not
identified yet. It is recorded as an open finding of the plan.

## GPU hangs during the series

The Ironlake GPU hung several times during this session; the kernel reset it each time and the
protocol's stop valve ended the affected series (the archive keeps the stopped attempts):

- During the first two starts, in the KDE panel (`plasmashell`) while VLC was playing hardware
  720p30, then repeatedly in `plasmashell` and `kwin_wayland` regardless of the player. The GPU
  stayed unreliable until a reboot; both stopped runs are discarded.
- After the reboot the ordinary scenes and the native Wayland hardware series ran without a hang.
  During the XWayland hardware series the GPU hung once in `Xwayland` while mpv (`x11egl`,
  VA-API) was playing; that series was repeated.

None of the hangs was in a Fastiplayer process; one of them (second start) happened while
Fastiplayer was playing, in the KDE panel's render thread, after the GPU had already been reset
three times.

## Limits

- Fastiplayer's A/V synchronisation is not measured (no A/V offset metric).
- GPU busy time per process is NOT TESTED: i915 on Gen5 exposes no `drm-engine-*` counters. The
  Render/3D column is global and descriptive.
- Lost-frame definitions differ: Fastiplayer unique frames handed to the surface (±1 is the
  window edge), mpv drop counters, VLC `frames lost`. None proves physical scanout.
- The 720p60 ordinary-scene rows compare Fastiplayer's hardware path with the references'
  software path (see the reference-mode note).
- The native Wayland 30 fps difference between days is unexplained (see above).
