# CPU parity on two laptops — ThinkPad T480s and AMD 780M, 2026-10-23

**On a 2018 ThinkPad T480s, Fastiplayer delivered every frame of all four
measured scenes. In each one it used less CPU and less GPU render time than mpv,
and in three of the four it was proven lower by the protocol's conservative
rule.** On the two 4K60 scenes the laptop is at its limit: mpv lost 37 % (HEVC,
hardware) and 56 % (AV1, software) of the frames, while Fastiplayer showed
1201 of 1201. Those two CPU results are therefore reported **with reference frame
losses** and are not a like-for-like efficiency claim. The plain result is that
Fastiplayer kept up where the reference did not.

| Scene (T480s) | Fastiplayer CPU | mpv CPU | CPU verdict | Render engine, Fastiplayer / mpv | Frames, Fastiplayer / mpv lost |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| H.264 1080p60, hardware | **8.5 %** | 9.5 % | **WIN** −0.96 pp | **6.0 %** / 26.2 % | 1201 / 0 |
| HEVC 4K60, hardware | **8.6 %** | 13.8 % | WIN ¹ −5.2 pp | **10.9 %** / 93.0 % | 1201 / **434–458** |
| H.264 1080p60, software | **83.8 %** | 88.2 % | INCONCLUSIVE −4.4 pp | **12.1 %** / 28.6 % | 1201 / 0 |
| AV1 4K60 (Big Buck Bunny), software | **364 %** | 619 % | WIN ¹ −255 pp | **21.9 %** / 85.7 % | 1201 / **671–693** |

CPU is the player's whole process tree as a share of **one** logical core (the
T480s has 8). ¹ With reference frame losses: mpv did less work than Fastiplayer
in the same window.

This report closes the measurement part of the CPU-parity plan
([`user/cpu-parity/`](../../user/cpu-parity/README.md), session 10; tracking goal
[#10](https://github.com/Bogdan7c/fastiplayer/issues/10)). The AMD 780M results it
summarizes are published in full in the
[2026-10-22 local report](local-cpu-parity-2026-10-22.md). Equal smoothness is not
established, and no PARITY label is emitted.

780M rows: hardware from session 06c (9 pairs, Fastiplayer `90321b08`), software
from session 08b (9 and 3 pairs, `e313720c`). The 780M report also covers
13 more hardware scenes and VP9/HEVC software; none of its scenes is WORSE.

**What changed since the first T480s measurement.** On 2026-09-05 this laptop
measured Fastiplayer at 16.9 % (H.264 1080p60) and 30.4 % (HEVC 4K60) process CPU
against VLC ([historical report](thinkpad-t480s.md)). The causes were found and
removed in sessions 01–08b: VA-API surfaces were recreated for every frame, every
frame was imported into Vulkan, the interface redrew on every vsync, idle
wake-ups ran every 2 ms, and software frames were copied on the CPU. The
September and October numbers differ in protocol, files, window and reference
player. They show the direction of the change, not a controlled before/after
ratio.

## Quality gate (diagnostic round)

The gate: Fastiplayer unique `(PTS, render generation, decoded generation)`
handoff identities ≥ 99 % of `source fps × log window`. The reference must
lose 0 frames; with `annotate`, its losses mark the scene instead of stopping
the series.

| Scene | Fastiplayer unique / expected | mpv lost frames (all attempts) | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| h264-1080p60 (hardware) | 1201 / 1200.6 | 0 in all 4 | PASS |
| hevc-4k60 (hardware) | 1201 / 1200.6 | **458, 434, 444, 441** | REFERENCE_LOSSES |
| h264-1080p60 (software) | 1201 / 1200.5 | 0 in all 10 | PASS |
| av1bbb-4k60 (software) | 1201 / 1201.7 | **671, 693, 679, 671** | REFERENCE_LOSSES |

- mpv's losses are `frame-drop-count`; `decoder-frame-drop-count` and
  `vo-delayed-frame-count` stayed 0.
- On HEVC 4K60, mpv's video engine (decoder) was 33 % busy, but its render
  engine was **93 %** busy (Fastiplayer: 11 %). mpv's `gpu-next` rendering of a
  4K frame does not fit the UHD 620 at 60 fps, and it drops frames at
  presentation.
- On AV1 4K60 both players decode in software. mpv's process used 615–623 % of
  a core and still dropped 56 % of the frames.
- These are surface handoffs, not physical scanout. The gate does not prove
  equal smoothness.

## CPU (quiet mode)

Metric: CPU of the player's cgroup tree (all threads and children), % of one
logical core, over the 20-second window.

- Δ = Fastiplayer − mpv; a negative value means Fastiplayer used less CPU.
- Interval: `mean ± (10 · sd / √n + 0.10)` pp.
- Status: **WIN** if the interval is below 0, **WORSE** if it is above 0, and
  INCONCLUSIVE otherwise.
- Exit bands: ±0.5 pp for hardware and ±1 pp for software, as on the 780M.

| Scene | Pairs | Fastiplayer %, mean (range) | mpv %, mean (range) | Δ mean, pp | Interval, pp | Verdict | Within exit band |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| h264-1080p60 (hardware) | 3 | 8.51 (8.28–8.73) | 9.48 (9.10–9.75) | −0.96 | [−1.76, −0.17] | **WIN** | yes |
| hevc-4k60 (hardware) ¹ | 3 | 8.59 (8.47–8.67) | 13.78 (13.59–13.92) | −5.20 | [−5.69, −4.71] | WIN ¹ | yes ¹ |
| h264-1080p60 (software) | 9 | 83.82 (82.40–85.60) | 88.22 (86.94–89.71) | −4.40 | [−9.17, +0.37] | INCONCLUSIVE | no |
| av1bbb-4k60 (software) ¹ | 3 | 363.8 (357.9–369.1) | 619.1 (615.5–623.1) | −255.3 | [−304.8, −205.9] | WIN ¹ | yes ¹ |

¹ With reference frame losses (quality gate above). The status is what the rule
computes; it is **not** a claim that Fastiplayer does the same work more
efficiently, because mpv presented fewer frames.

The software H.264 1080p60 mean is in Fastiplayer's favour in every one of the
9 pairs (differences −2.0 … −6.3 pp), but mpv's run-to-run spread keeps the
conservative interval (t = 10) wider than the ±1 pp band. That is the same
limitation the 780M series met at 60 fps.

## Memory (quiet mode)

| Scene | RSS, Fastiplayer mean / max | RSS, mpv mean | PSS, Fastiplayer / mpv | GPU memory, simple sum: Fastiplayer / mpv | GPU memory, shared counted once: Fastiplayer / mpv |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| h264-1080p60 (hardware) | 95 / 98 | 212 | 82 / 184 | 199 / 101 | 127 / 74 |
| hevc-4k60 (hardware) | 94 / 94 | 307 | 81 / 279 | 602 / 344 | 330 / 305 |
| h264-1080p60 (software) | 174 / 178 | 224 | 161 / 197 | 69 / 171 | 69 / 171 |
| av1bbb-4k60 (software) | 378 / 391 | 514 | 365 / 486 | 129 / 268 | 129 / 268 |

MiB, means over the quiet attempts.

- **RSS and PSS** are the process-memory metrics used in every earlier report.
  Fastiplayer is lower on all four scenes: 2–3× on the hardware path.
- **GPU memory** is memory the GPU driver holds for the player's DRM clients
  (i915 region `system0`, resident). Its pages are not in RSS: Fastiplayer's
  RSS was 95 MiB while its clients held about 200 MiB on hardware H.264.
  - The *simple sum* is the protocol's `gtt` column: it adds every client. On
    the hardware path each player has two clients that see the same decoded
    surfaces: the VA-API decoder exports them and the Vulkan renderer imports
    them. The sum counts those buffers twice.
  - The *shared counted once* column is an estimate: `Σ(resident − shared) +
    max(shared)`. It assumes that all buffers marked shared in the tree are one
    set.
  - Fastiplayer keeps a bounded pool of decoded 4K surfaces for smooth seeking
    and ordered presentation (~276 MiB at 4K on this laptop); mpv's is smaller.
  - On the hardware path the estimate is therefore **higher for Fastiplayer**:
    330 vs 305 MiB on HEVC 4K60 and 127 vs 74 MiB on H.264 1080p60. Process plus
    GPU memory is still lower: about 424 vs 612 MiB and 222 vs 287 MiB.
- Software frames that Fastiplayer imports into Vulkan are counted in both RSS
  and GPU memory while a copy is in flight, which overstates its total by a few
  frames. Software scenes have a single DRM client, so both GPU columns
  coincide.

## Collector fixes

- **Thermal `ENODATA`.** The September report listed a collector limitation: a
  thermal zone answering `ENODATA` aborted collection on another host. It was
  already fixed in session 06. Unreadable zones are stored as unavailable with a
  reason, and the protocol's condition snapshot treats them as optional. Both
  collectors' tests pass on this laptop, including its Wi-Fi zone.
- **i915 naming (this session).** The local protocol was written on amdgpu and
  read the render engine as `gfx` and GPU memory as `drm-memory-gtt`. On i915
  the first T480s summaries therefore showed "gfx engine counter missing" and a
  GTT of 0, although the raw per-client counters (`render`, `video`,
  `drm-resident-system0`) were recorded correctly.
  - `host.py` now maps both drivers (`RENDER_ENGINE_NAMES`, `GPU_MEMORY_FIELDS`;
    amdgpu names keep priority).
  - The analysis recomputes GPU memory from the preserved raw samples. The
    measurements did not change and were not repeated.
  - Tests cover the real i915 counter shapes, the amdgpu priority, and
    recomputation equal to the stored amdgpu summary; reverting the mapping
    makes the i915 tests fail.

## Limitations

- One laptop per cohort, XWayland only, 20-second windows, 3–9 pairs. Native
  Wayland, battery power, long sessions, seeking and startup time were not
  measured. Results do not transfer to other GPUs or drivers.
- On both 4K60 scenes the reference lost frames, so the CPU verdict there does
  not compare equal work. What the scenes show is that Fastiplayer presented all
  frames on this laptop while mpv, in its default configuration, did not.
- Different decoders and renderers: hardware mpv uses VA-API +
  `gpu-next`/Vulkan, while Fastiplayer uses VA-API + DMA-BUF + its own Vulkan
  renderer. In software, the distribution FFmpeg 9.0.2 is compared with the
  vendored FFmpeg 8.1.3 / dav1d 1.5.4. This is a default-behaviour comparison
  of two whole players, not of one decoder implementation.
- Fastiplayer's AV1 thread policy (at most 6 dav1d threads above 1080p, 2 frames
  in flight; session 08) was checked here outside the protocol: 8 threads cost
  +75…85 pp of CPU with the same 960 of 960 frames on the heavy segment.
- Quality evidence is limited to surface-handoff identities and mpv's own
  counters, not physical scanout or perceived smoothness.
- The excluded first series is described above; its raw data stays local
  because runtime logs contain local paths.
- Sanitized per-attempt evidence, both series with plans, tool hashes and
  summaries: [`t480s-cpu-parity-2026-10-23.json.gz`](t480s-cpu-parity-2026-10-23.json.gz).
  Raw logs, screenshots and per-sample observations are kept outside Git.

## Reproduction

```sh
cargo build --release -p app-egui --locked
cc -Wall -Wextra -Werror -O2 docs/benchmarks/tools/local/window_owner.c \
  -lxcb -lxcb-res -o /absolute/artifacts/window-owner
python3 -m unittest discover -s docs/benchmarks/tools/local -p 'test_*.py'
python3 docs/benchmarks/tools/local/make_matrix.py /absolute/master-4k60.mp4 \
  /absolute/benchmark-matrix --start 300 --duration 45 --only h264-1080p60 --only hevc-4k60
python3 docs/benchmarks/tools/local/run_series.py /absolute/plan-hw.json /absolute/new-series-hw
python3 docs/benchmarks/tools/local/run_series.py /absolute/plan-sw.json /absolute/new-series-sw
python3 docs/benchmarks/tools/local/analyze.py /absolute/new-series-hw
```

Plan fields (common): `protocol_version` 3, `warmup_seconds` 12, `duration_seconds`
20, `interval_seconds` 1, `display_size` [1920, 1080], `diagnostic_repetitions` 1.
Hardware: `reference_losses` `annotate`. Software: `decode` `software`,
`exit_band_percentage_points` 1.0, `reference_losses` `annotate`. The players are
`fastiplayer` and `mpv`. The exact plans (without paths) are in the evidence file.
